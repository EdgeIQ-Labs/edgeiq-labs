Frequently asked questions

What is SPF and why do I need it?
SPF (Sender Policy Framework) is a DNS TXT record that specifies which mail servers are authorized to send email on behalf of your domain. Without SPF, receiving servers can't verify if an email claiming to be from you is legitimate, making your domain vulnerable to spoofing and increasing the chance your emails land in spam.
What is DMARC and how does it protect my domain?
DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting & Conformance) builds on SPF and DKIM to tell receiving servers what to do when an email fails authentication. It also provides aggregate reports so you can see who's sending email as your domain and catch spoofing attempts before they damage your reputation.
How long does it take for DNS changes to propagate?
DNS changes typically propagate within 1–24 hours, though many resolvers update within minutes. TTL (Time To Live) values on your existing records affect this — lower TTLs mean faster propagation. Use tools like dig or online DNS checkers to verify your new records are live.
Can I have multiple SPF records on one domain?
NO. Having multiple SPF TXT records on a single domain violates the SPF specification (RFC 7208) and causes a PermError result, which breaks email delivery entirely. You must combine all mechanisms into a single SPF record. If you exceed the 10-DNS-lookup limit, use SPF flattening services.
