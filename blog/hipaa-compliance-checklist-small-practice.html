HIPAA Compliance Checklist for Small Healthcare Practices

HIPAA Healthcare Compliance

You run a small practice. You don't have a CISO or a compliance department. But OCR doesn't care about your headcount when they show up after a breach. Here's the technical safeguards checklist that gets you audit-ready without enterprise budgets.

HIPAA's Security Rule has three safeguard categories: administrative, physical, and technical. Most small practices handle administrative (policies, training) and physical (locked doors, clean desk) reasonably well. Technical safeguards are where gaps live and where breaches happen. This guide focuses there.

For the full SMB compliance picture including PCI and general frameworks, see our SMB compliance checklist.

Access Controls (§164.312(a)(1))

Every person who touches PHI needs a unique user ID. Shared accounts are an automatic audit finding.

✅ Unique User Identification

Each staff member has their own login. No shared "frontdesk" or "admin" accounts. Terminated employees are disabled within 24 hours.

✅ Automatic Logoff

Sessions timeout after 15 minutes of inactivity. Workstations lock automatically. Screen savers require password to dismiss.

✅ Emergency Access Procedure

Documented process for accessing PHI during system outages. Break-glass accounts exist with audit logging enabled.

If you're using cloud-hosted EHR or practice management software, verify your vendor provides these controls natively. Our SaaS permission audit guide covers how to check.

Audit Controls (§164.312(b))

You must be able to answer "who accessed what, when" for any PHI record. This is where most small practices fail during audits.

✅ Activity Logging Enabled

All systems handling PHI log user access, modifications, deletions, and exports. Logs retained for minimum 6 years per HIPAA retention rules.

✅ Log Monitoring Active

Logs aren't just collected, they're reviewed. Weekly monitoring routines catch anomalies before they become breaches. Wazuh SIEM automates this — see our Compliance Shield service for managed monitoring.

✅ Audit Trail Integrity

Logs cannot be modified or deleted by regular users. Centralized log storage with write-once retention prevents tampering.

Integrity Controls (§164.312(c)(1))

PHI must be protected from improper alteration or destruction.

✅ File Integrity Monitoring

Critical system files and PHI databases monitored for unauthorized changes. Alerts fire on unexpected modifications.

✅ Backup Verification

Daily automated backups with quarterly restore testing. Backups encrypted at rest and in transit. Offsite copies maintained. See our ransomware readiness checklist for backup best practices.

✅ Anti-Malware Protection

Endpoint protection on all workstations and servers. Real-time scanning enabled. Signature updates automated.

Transmission Security (§164.312(e)(1))

PHI in transit must be encrypted. Email is the most common violation vector for small practices.

✅ Email Encryption

All outbound email containing PHI uses TLS encryption. Patient portal preferred over email for PHI delivery. Verify your email provider supports enforced TLS — run our DMARC checker and SPF analyzer to confirm authentication is configured correctly.

✅ VPN for Remote Access

Staff accessing PHI remotely use encrypted VPN connections. No direct RDP or VNC over the public internet.

✅ Website SSL/TLS

Patient-facing portals enforce HTTPS with modern cipher suites. Run our SSL checker to verify certificate validity and configuration.

Business Associate Agreements (§164.314(a))

Every vendor who touches PHI needs a signed BAA. This includes your EHR vendor, billing service, IT support, cloud hosting provider, and even your email marketing platform if it handles patient communications.

✅ BAA Inventory Complete

List every vendor with PHI access. Signed BAAs on file for each. Annual review scheduled. Our vendor BAA verification guide walks through the process.

✅ Cloud Provider BAA

If PHI lives in the cloud (AWS, Azure, GCP, or managed hosting), the provider has signed a BAA covering your specific services. Standard Terms of Service ≠ BAA.

Our third-party security verification checklist covers vendor risk assessment beyond just the BAA.

Risk Assessment (§164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A))

This is the foundation everything else builds on. OCR expects a documented risk assessment updated annually or when significant changes occur.

✅ Risk Assessment Documented

Written assessment identifies threats, vulnerabilities, and likelihood/impact for each PHI system. Updated within last 12 months.

✅ Risk Management Plan

Each identified risk has a mitigation strategy, owner, and timeline. Acceptable risks are formally accepted with justification.

✅ Security Policy Framework

Written policies cover acceptable use, incident response, contingency operations, and workforce sanctions. Staff acknowledge annually. Our policy framework guide provides templates.

The 30-Day Sprint: Getting Audit-Ready Fast

If an audit notice landed on your desk tomorrow, here's the prioritized sprint:

  • Week 1: Complete risk assessment, inventory all PHI systems, verify BAAs
  • Week 2: Enable audit logging everywhere, configure automatic logoff, test backups
  • Week 3: Deploy endpoint protection, encrypt email, verify SSL/TLS on all portals
  • Week 4: Write policies, train staff, document everything, schedule annual review

Common Small Practice Mistakes

  • Assuming EHR vendor handles everything — they handle their platform, not your workstations, email, or network
  • Skipping backup restore tests — backups that can't restore are worse than no backups because they create false confidence
  • Ignoring business associates — your billing company's breach is YOUR breach if the BAA is missing
  • Treating compliance as one-time — annual risk assessments and ongoing monitoring are requirements, not suggestions
  • No incident response plan — when (not if) something happens, having a written procedure saves days of chaos. See our incident response checklist.
