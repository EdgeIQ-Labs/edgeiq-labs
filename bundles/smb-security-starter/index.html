Two small subscriptions that cover the checks most small teams forget: whether a certificate is about to expire, and whether your email setup looks like a phishing risk. Billed separately, so neither one locks you in.

Why these two together

Essentials gives you the broad monthly picture: SSL, uptime, email headers, and a report you can actually read. CertWatch does one narrow job every day — it watches the expiry date and emails you while you still have weeks to act. A monthly report is useful for trends; it is too slow for a certificate that expires on a Friday.

Neither tool changes your DNS or needs an agent installed. You register a domain and read the results.

Who this is for

  • A team of one to ten with no dedicated security staff
  • A site on a wildcard certificate, where one expiry takes down every subdomain
  • Someone who has been renewing certificates by hand and wants a warning first
  • A business that wants the basics watched without a daily dashboard habit

What it does not cover

This starter is monitoring, not a full security program. It does not parse DMARC aggregate reports, run a compliance audit, or respond to incidents. FAQ

What is included in the SMB Security Starter?
SMB Essentials and CertWatch Pro, bought as two separate subscriptions. Essentials covers SSL monitoring, uptime checks, email header analysis, and a monthly security report. CertWatch Pro checks certificate expiry daily and emails you at 30, 14, 7, and 1 day before expiry.
Is there one price for the bundle?
No. There is no single checkout for both. SMB Essentials is $29 per month or $278 per year, with a 14-day trial. CertWatch Pro is $1 per month. Each has its own checkout link and can be cancelled independently.
Do I need both?
If you already automate Let's Encrypt renewals and watch them, Essentials alone may be enough. If certificates are renewed by hand or sit on a wildcard, CertWatch Pro gives you a warning before browsers do.
Does this include DMARC report analysis?
No. For DMARC, SPF, and DKIM record monitoring, see Inbox Shield. For a walkthrough of what monitoring costs at each tier, see our DMARC monitoring pricing comparison.
